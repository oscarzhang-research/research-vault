Thesis in one sentence

I think the market currently severely underestimates SanDisk. Given NBM's rising coverage of NAND bit supply (which both protects the price floor and increases actual guaranteed revenue), the ever-growing demand for enterprise SSDs from AI data centers, the rapid progress of HBF, and SanDisk's rising revenue mix toward AI data centers, SanDisk's downside protection against falling prices has been dramatically strengthened, and the durability of its profitability has improved in a way it never has before.

Core logic

  1. Historically, the storage industry (especially NAND) could not escape strong cyclicality. This is fundamentally a path dependency: shortage emerges — profits explode — capex restarts — an 18–30 month stall — a bit flood — price collapse — production cuts plus capex cuts — supply bottoms out — back to the start. Looking at the past decade as one cycle, the fundamental reason storage (especially NAND) could not escape this pattern is that there was no structural demand change. The 2017 cycle was driven by a supply shortfall as NAND transitioned from 2D to 3D. The 2021 pandemic-era cycle was driven by a surge in electronic devices during COVID. Neither cycle was caused by a structural shift in demand — both, at their core, were supply-side shortages.

    In the current AI era, a genuinely cost-insensitive buyer has emerged: AI data centers, led by the top-five hyperscalers. This is a true structural demand change. Cloud providers (AI data centers) will absorb an enormous amount of storage. According to Morgan Stanley's NAND supply-demand model, AI-related NAND demand will nearly triple from 205EB in 2025 to 609EB in 2027, rising from 18% to 41% of total demand. This will create roughly a 9% supply–demand gap in the global NAND market (a high-end AI GPU requires roughly 16TB of TLC/QLC flash; an NVL72 rack requires roughly 1,152TB of NAND).

    Another data point: in 2026, enterprise SSD will for the first time become the largest NAND application segment. Enterprise products' share of total NAND revenue will rise from roughly 45% in 2024 to roughly 62% in 2026, while consumer/client shrinks from 55% to 38%. As AI data centers keep upgrading, agentic AI demand keeps exploding, Nvidia's architecture keeps advancing, hyperscalers' TPU compute keeps improving, and cloud ROI keeps improving, NAND demand will keep growing going forward, and enterprise SSD's share of total NAND demand will keep rising.

    On top of this, there has also been a notable shift on the supply side this time (though as I've said before, supply-side changes on their own will only push the end of the cycle back a little). Because HBM is both explosively profitable and more sustainable, Samsung, Micron, and SK Hynix are shifting as many resources as possible toward HBM — and these three also happen to be nearly the largest NAND producers, so the supply-side pressure is being transmitted into NAND (though it's worth noting NAND is not the first beneficiary of this AI wave). According to the latest data from major investment banks, HBM's current margins are far below DRAM's — an abnormal state that will gradually normalize as new long-term contracts get signed (normally, HBM margins should be higher than DRAM's). In other words, HBM prices will keep rising (JPMorgan estimates HBM prices could double by 2027). This will push the supply side to keep tilting more resources toward HBM, extending the duration of the NAND supply–demand imbalance (NAND bit-supply growth in 2026 is 16.5%, versus a terrifying 45% growth rate on the eve of the 2018 cycle collapse, with 25% being the historical norm).

  2. NBM's protection for SanDisk is severely underestimated. According to SanDisk's latest quarterly report, the company has signed 10 agreements covering 8 data center and edge customers, with minimum committed revenue of $93.9 billion and a weighted-average term of 4 years. That implies an astonishing $23.5 billion in guaranteed minimum annual revenue for SanDisk.

    According to the latest earnings call and management commentary, these long-term agreements bring $16.5 billion in financial guarantees (deposits/collateral). Management estimates NBM will cover half of bit shipments by 2027 (implying current coverage is below half) and two-thirds of shipped bits will be locked in via long-term agreements by 2028.

    NBM combines fixed pricing with floating pricing, and the floating portion has both a floor and a ceiling. Per management, even at the floor price, SanDisk's NBM gross margin can still reach 80%, and if spot prices keep rising, SanDisk still captures the upside.

    For a company like SanDisk, which the market has historically understood as strongly cyclical, NBM is enormously significant. It provides multiple layers of protection for revenue and profit — including but not limited to prepayments, third-party derivative guarantees, quarterly purchase commitments, and automatic default-triggered protection mechanisms — which dramatically raises SanDisk's floor-level profitability.

    NBM currently covers less than 50% of bits, and the minimum annual revenue this already generates is a staggering $23.5 billion (note: this is the floor). Even on the simplest calculation, SanDisk's 2027 revenue will very likely exceed $50 billion (given that the portion without long-term agreements will likely keep seeing price increases, and NBM locks in less than half of shipped bits). Gross margin will likely not fall below 80–85% (management guided next quarter's gross margin at 84%, versus 84.6% this quarter), and net margin will also be an extraordinary 75%+.

    By 2028, SanDisk's NBM will cover two-thirds of shipped bits. Because NBM itself imposes strict constraints on buyers, SanDisk could gain extremely strong floor-level protection in 2028, and 2028 revenue could even exceed 2027 (thanks to the 80% gross-margin floor plus continued shipment growth).

    NBM may not change the cycle, but it fundamentally reconstructs SanDisk's business model — turning SanDisk into something close to a landlord collecting rent with near-total certainty.

  3. SanDisk itself is undervalued, especially in this AI wave. When structural demand shifts trigger a reconstruction of a strongly cyclical company's business model, that company shouldn't be valued using the traditional valuation framework.

    By simple calculation, SanDisk's 2027 revenue will exceed $50 billion and Non-GAAP net income will reach $35 billion. At 9x forward P/E, SanDisk's market cap would reach $315 billion; at 10x forward P/E, $350 billion. Under a DCF approach, SanDisk would accumulate enormous cash flow by 2028 — a rough calculation suggests $60–80 billion in cumulative cash flow by 2028, nearly a third of the current market cap.

    Given that SanDisk's floor-level protection is now completely different from the past — the floor has been significantly raised, and it will keep rising further over time as NBM coverage increases — assigning a 9–10x forward P/E is not excessive (in the old strong-cycle era, the market typically assigned storage companies only 5–7x peak-earnings forward multiples). And 2027 may not even be SanDisk's earnings peak (though that's the current market consensus).

    Right now, SanDisk's market cap is still being valued the old strong-cycle way. There are two ways SanDisk can re-rate: first, by proving 2027 is not the earnings peak (or at least not much higher than 2028); second, by proving its earnings durability is far superior to the old strong-cycle era — i.e., that 2028, 2029, 2030, and beyond do not see a significant decline in profitability.

  4. SanDisk's customer base is undergoing a significant shift (based on the latest quarterly data): SanDisk's data center segment grew 103% QoQ and more than 10x YoY, generating $3 billion in revenue and accounting for 38% of total bit volume, with continued high growth expected.

    As I've noted, AI data centers are cost-insensitive buyers with low price sensitivity, so a rising mix of data center business is a clear positive for SanDisk across any time horizon. This will further raise and stabilize SanDisk's margins, especially as pricing gains in consumer electronics approach their limit (SanDisk's latest quarterly report shows its consumer electronics revenue has already fallen by 30%).

    SanDisk's currently blowout results have occurred even as consumer electronics is notably declining, which further supports the view that SanDisk is undervalued: if NAND price increases slow or even reverse going forward, consumer electronics revenue should actually trend upward (and edge business revenue may not even decline, since rising shipment volumes of phones and PCs could offset the impact of falling prices).

    Of course, SanDisk's current focus is clearly on the AI data center business — where incremental volume and margins are highest — as well as the edge business. And NAND still has two enormous incremental markets ahead: robotics and autonomous driving. Given that SanDisk's current valuation already prices in part of a downside scenario, I think there is significant room for re-rating ahead, driven by price-insensitive buyers like AI data centers, robotics, and autonomous driving, or by these huge incremental markets.

  5. On August 3, 2026, SanDisk and SK Hynix, through the Open Compute Project (OCP), formally released the first open HBF technical specification — a result achieved only about six months after standardization work began. Google and Tenstorrent have already joined the alliance to participate in technical validation.

    I view HBF as an important call option that could reshape NAND's position. Once the standard is established and successfully enters AI data centers, NAND will insert itself as a critical layer between HBM and DRAM, and the revenue from this layer could be extremely substantial. The HBM market is forecast at roughly $155 billion in 2027; as the next-fastest-response storage layer below HBM, even if HBF captures only a third of HBM's market size, that would still exceed $50 billion. This is a segment SanDisk would have essentially to itself — pure incremental upside.

Key assumptions and tracking signals

  • ☐Whether NAND contract price gains in 2026Q3 exceed 10%–15%
  • ☐Whether NAND contract price increases can extend into 2027 (especially watching enterprise SSDs used in AI data centers)
  • ☐Whether NAND inventory buildup exceeds 4 weeks or more
  • ☐Whether major US tech giants (the top-five hyperscalers + new cloud players + top LLM companies) continue to increase capex (fundamentally, this is about whether demand keeps growing rapidly)
  • ☐Whether YMTC can ramp production at scale within the next year (or few quarters), with capacity expansion arriving earlier than expected, especially in enterprise SSD
  • ☐Whether Samsung, SK Hynix, and Micron — the three storage leaders — expand NAND capacity at an extremely fast pace, causing the supply-demand inflection point to arrive earlier (whether the 2027 supply-demand balance eases sooner than the market expects)
  • ☐Whether SanDisk's HBF can achieve smooth mass production (2027–2028)
  • ☐Whether SanDisk's NBM coverage can hit management's targets (50% by 2027, 67% by 2028)
  • ☐Whether cloud providers' ROI can continue to improve (or at least not decline)

What would prove me wrong

If any of the following occurs, I'll admit I was wrong:

  1. NAND contract price gains in 2026Q3 come in below 10%, and the price rally ends earlier than expected.
  2. Supply discipline among the major storage makers breaks down for whatever reason, and supply increases arrive earlier and larger than expected.
  3. Major US tech giants (the top-five hyperscalers + new cloud players + top LLM companies) stop increasing capex (or increase it by less than the market expects).
  4. NBM coverage falls short of expectations.
  5. SanDisk's HBF commercialization progresses slower than expected.
  6. Customers breach contracts because the gap between spot prices and NBM contract prices becomes too large.

Where I differ from consensus

Consensus view: SanDisk is a legacy storage manufacturer, and NAND's resilience is far weaker than DRAM's. Given strong cyclicality plus NAND's substitutability, the market assigns SanDisk a low valuation.

Why I differ: AI-driven growth will keep NAND demand imbalanced for a considerable stretch of time. AI data centers are cost-insensitive buyers. Capacity expansion is falling far short of demand. NBM protects SanDisk's profit floor — a floor that now even exceeds past cycle peaks. And HBF could reshape NAND's position entirely. SanDisk deserves a materially higher valuation.
