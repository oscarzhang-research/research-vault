Thesis in one sentence

The market is severely underestimating SK hynix's value. The reasoning here is largely the same as in the other three memory-company notes I've written — the only difference is that SK hynix carries a higher HBM market share, which gives it more stable, more visible revenue and a wider moat. As the market re-prices HBM, SK hynix's revenue and operating profit could surge even further.

Core logic

1. Let me first lay out the shared core logic, since I've already written three similar notes and the underlying logic doesn't change: a likely long-term supply–demand imbalance, plus HBM's high barriers to entry (no new competitors), plus long-term agreements sharply lifting the cycle trough, plus an extremely clear, explosive earnings outlook for 2026–2027.

2. SK hynix holds close to 60% of the HBM market, and that is its biggest moat relative to the other memory companies. It means a substantial share of its revenue is tied to the top-five hyperscalers in the U.S., which significantly reduces its revenue's exposure to broader market swings — unlike consumer electronics. That said, this also carries real risk, including whether SK hynix can sustain close to 60% HBM market share over the long run, and whether its profit ceiling at peak might actually be lower than expected. My own caveat: in recent quarters HBM's per-bit margin has actually trailed traditional DRAM, and because HBM is typically tied to long-term agreements, its upside is capped.

3. I estimate SK hynix's FY2026 revenue at around ₩330 trillion, with operating profit around ₩250 trillion, and FY2027 operating profit around ₩375 trillion. FY2026 revenue and operating profit are already extremely clear at this point. For FY2027 I'm assuming 50% growth — that is, a 15% increase in shipments combined with a 30% increase in contract prices — which doesn't front-load an overly optimistic scenario and is a fairly rational estimate. Applying a 10x multiple to FY2027 operating profit gives a market cap of roughly $2.2 trillion, or a share price of around ₩4.15 million. Factoring in the Korean discount, my target price comes to roughly ₩3,800,000. Adding another 15% margin of safety on top, my final target price for SK hynix is around ₩3,200,000. (I've covered the detailed valuation methodology in my other notes; I'll add a full Excel valuation model later.)

Key assumptions and tracking signals

  • ☐Whether SK hynix can sustain its current close-to-60% HBM market share
  • ☐Whether SK hynix will subsequently roll out a detailed shareholder return plan
  • ☐Whether SK hynix's long-term agreement coverage ratio and committed volumes can keep rising
  • ☐Whether the Korean stock market can gradually stabilize (deleveraging caused major volatility back in July)
  • ☐Whether the U.S. and Korea can reach agreement on how memory profits should be distributed

What would prove me wrong

What would make me admit I'm wrong:

  1. SK hynix's HBM market share falls below 50%
  2. SK hynix fails to return 50% or more of its FCF to shareholders going forward
  3. SK hynix's long-term agreement coverage ratio and committed volumes fail to keep rising
  4. Korea's stock market deleveraging process doesn't conclude and volatility remains severe
  5. The U.S. and Korea remain unable to reach agreement on memory profit distribution

Where I differ from consensus

What the market thinks: SK hynix is a traditional cyclical stock.

Why I differ: memory is a strategic resource of the AI infrastructure era, and SK hynix is the core player holding the single most important strategic resource of that era — it's the HBM leader. Combined with a new industry ecosystem the memory sector hasn't seen in years, it deserves a higher valuation that breaks from the traditional cyclical-stock valuation framework.
