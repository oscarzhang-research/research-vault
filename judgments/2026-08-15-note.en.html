Position Management
Written 2026-08-15 and published unedited. Translated from the Chinese original — see the 中 link above.

What I got right

  1. AI remains the main thread of this bull market; short-term noise should be ignored.
  2. The underlying logic for memory hasn't changed. What AI development lacks most is compute, and what limits compute the most is memory (the memory wall). Memory has shifted from a strong-cyclical commodity into an AI strategic material (even though its commodity properties may not have changed). All AI hardware is really a product of this AI supercycle, and since it's all cyclical, it all faces the same systemic risk. Memory's valuation should be re-rated upward, especially given that future earnings persistence could rise substantially — that is, the floor has been lifted up to the peak level of past cycles.

What I got wrong

  1. Global equity market correlation risk. Deleveraging in Korea triggered violent swings in June and July, cutting many tech stocks (including memory names) in half — the magnitude and speed of the drop exceeded what I imagined.
  2. The valuation safety margin on AI tech stocks was too thin — share prices had likely priced in far too much optimism, so bad macro news (even baseless rumors) could trigger an unwarranted sell-off.
  3. I didn't factor in the macro environment at all.

The common cause behind the misses

  1. I over-focused on fundamentals and nearly ignored every other factor — the macro environment, global equity market correlation, and external leverage ratios.
  2. I lacked a margin-of-safety mindset, leaving too thin a profit cushion.

What I'm changing

  • ☐No longer use any leveraged instruments (margin financing or leveraged ETFs).
  • ☐No longer add to positions using unrealized gains.
  • ☐The initial entry must not exceed one-third of the intended total position.
  • ☐Reinforce margin-of-safety discipline — if the price hasn't reached a safe zone, I'd rather miss the move.
