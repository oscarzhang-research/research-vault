HOW I GET FROM A THESIS TO A NAME

The funnel

Five steps. Each one narrows the field, and each one has to be answered before the next is worth asking.

01

Demand thesis

Huang's layered framing of compute demand — the idea that AI spending is not one market but a stack, and each layer pulls the one beneath it.

MY READIf the top of the stack keeps spending, the constraint moves down until it lands on something physical.
02

Where it binds

Not the loudest bottleneck — the one that cannot be relieved by spending money faster. Physical constraints outlast financial ones.

MY READMemory, and HBM specifically. The same bit count consumes roughly 4× the wafer area, and that is physics, not capex.
03

Highest-rent link

A bottleneck is not automatically profitable. The question is which link captures the pricing power the shortage creates, and which merely passes it along.

MY READThe memory makers themselves, not the equipment vendors or the packagers. A handful of players, no new entrant this cycle, and long-term agreements that put a floor under price.
04

The names, on six axes

Read each company on six dimensions — what the business actually sells, management's execution record, moat depth and durability, growth runway, whether the valuation is defensible, and the full risk picture. Not as a checklist, but to find which single dimension decides the outcome.

MY READFor all four the deciding dimension turned out to be the same one — whether the pricing floor holds — which is why the models spend most of their effort there.
05

The deciding variable

Once the deciding variable is identified, the rest is arithmetic: build the model, state the assumption, write down what would falsify it.

MY READSupply and demand bit by bit — the gap arithmetic — plus a three-scenario DCF per name, every assumption tied to a historical anchor and a falsification signal.

Steps 1–3 are where most of the thinking happens and almost none of the writing. By the time a name reaches step 4 the interesting question is usually already settled — which is why a judgment on this site tends to be short, and the model behind it long.
